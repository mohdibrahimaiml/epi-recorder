Decision Record · free and open source

Every AI decision needs a Decision Record.

One readable record per decision: what the model saw, what it decided and why, which model answered, who approved it, and what happened next.

Logs answer what the system did. Traces answer where time went. Dashboards answer whether a control exists. A Decision Record answers the question a customer, auditor or insurer actually asks.

What is inside

Eight fields a reviewer can use.

01 · Goal

The case

What the agent was asked to do, for whom.

02 · Steps

What it saw

The inputs, tool calls and results along the way.

03 · Decision

The outcome

What was decided, in plain language.

04 · Reasons

Why

The rationale recorded at the time, not reconstructed later.

05 · Rules

What applied

The policy, limit or eligibility rule behind the decision.

06 · Model

Who answered

Which model and version responded, and when.

07 · Approval

Who signed off

The human approval, with their key and timestamp — or that none was required.

08 · Outcome

What happened next

The action taken or the customer result.

Completeness

The ten-question completeness checklist.

Score one point per question you can answer from the record itself, without asking the vendor to log in or search another system.

9–10: a Decision Record. 6–8: close, but reasons, approval or outcome are incomplete. 3–5: observability data. 0–2: logs.

Where this fits

A record is the product. The review is the service.

If a customer is asking for the record behind a decision, that is the AI vendor review engagement: one workflow, thirty days, a Decision Record per case, and a review pack. If you only want to produce and read records yourself, the recorder and verifier are free and open source.

Technical details — sealing, keys, the customer witness and offline verification — live on the trust model page.

Working in insurance? The NAIC's questions to insurers and these eight fields line up closely. See the minimum AI decision record for insurance AI vendors.
