For reviewers

You received an AI Decision Record.
Check it yourself.

For the bank, insurer or lender whose AI vendor sends .epi Decision Records. Checking one is free, runs on your own machine, and needs no account with EPI or with the vendor.

Check a file in your browser One-page reviewer brief

What you receive

  • One file per case. What the AI decided, the principal reasons, the rules and data it relied on, which model answered, and who approved it.
  • Your own witness log. When the vendor seals each case, a short fingerprint of it arrives in a log that you keep. It holds no prompts and no personal data, and the vendor cannot change your copy.

Check one file — in a browser

Open epilabs.org/verify and drop the .epi file, plus your witness log (received.jsonl) if you have one. Nothing is uploaded. You will see whether the file has changed since it was sealed and, with the log, whether it is the version you were told about on the day — a rewritten and re-signed file reads “This is not the version your witness recorded.”

Check it properly — on your machine

The vendor sends you their public key, and each approver sends theirs. Confirm the fingerprints by phone, not by email, then:

$ pip install -U "epi-recorder>=4.9.0"
$ epi keys trust vendor.pub --name vendor --fingerprint <from the vendor>
$ epi approvers add approver.pub --name alice@bank.com --fingerprint <from the approver>
$ epi verify case.epi --policy strict --require-human-approval --witness-log received.jsonl

Needs epi-recorder 4.9.0 or newer; check with epi --version. Recording, verification and the commands above need no account and no licence.

DECISION: PASS means the file is unchanged, it was sealed by the vendor key you pinned, a person you pinned signed off, and it matches your log. Anything else tells you exactly what is missing. Details: reviewer brief.

The periodic review

For a quarter’s worth of cases, epi oversight report reads the files and your own log and writes one page: decisions by outcome, every model version that answered, who approved what, and the gaps — cases reported to you but never delivered, cases you expected that were never recorded, and files that don’t match your log. Available from epi-recorder 4.9 (how it works).

$ epi oversight report --cases ./received --log received.jsonl \
    --since 2026-07-01 --until 2026-09-30 --out oversight-q3.html

What each answer means

From epi-recorder 4.10, three more checks answer the questions a bank or insurer asks about an AI vendor. Each runs offline, on your machine, against your own witness log.

1. Was anything held back?

epi witness audit received.jsonl
AnswerMeansExit
cleanEvery numbered record in your chain, up to the latest heartbeat, was reported. Numbering is per customer: another customer's log is a different sequence. Without a heartbeat, only the delivered records are checked.0
MISSING #NRecord N was sealed but never reported to you, in your chain. Ask why.1
FORKTwo different records claim the same number.1
CHAIN BREAKA record doesn’t link to the one before it.1
TAIL GAPThe vendor’s heartbeat says more records exist than you received.1
APPROVAL REUSEDOne approval was used for more than one action.1
BURNED #NThe vendor says record N failed while sealing. Not verifiable; worth asking about if it grows.0 (1 with --strict)
NEW CHAINA new numbering started for a known key (new machine or reset).0

Numbers are per customer. A gap in one customer’s log is not a gap in another’s.

2. Did an approved version make this decision?

epi verify decision.epi --witness-log received.jsonl --release-dir releases/ --require-release --min-approvals 2
AnswerMeans
MATCHESApproved release, sealed first, approved before use, and every observed component matches.
DRIFTSomething that ran differs from the release.
UNDECLAREDSomething ran that the release doesn’t list (for example a second model).
NOT FULLY OBSERVEDDeclared in the release but EPI couldn’t see it at runtime. Never counted as a match.
APPROVED AFTER USEApprovals exist, but were made after this decision.
UNAPPROVED RELEASENot enough approvals. Your --min-approvals wins over the vendor’s number.
RELEASE SEALED AFTER DECISIONThe release was created after the decision.
DIRTY RELEASEBuilt from uncommitted code.
AMBIGUOUS RELEASETwo release files claim the same id. EPI won’t pick one.

Default: warnings. With --require-release: any answer other than MATCHES fails.

3. Did a person approve before it ran?

epi verify decision.epi --witness-log received.jsonl --require-pre-action-approval
AnswerMeans
APPROVED BEFORE ACTIONA pinned person signed this exact action, and your log received the approval before the execution.
ACTION BEFORE APPROVALYour log received the execution first.
ACTION WITHOUT APPROVALThe action needed approval and has none.
DENIED BUT EXECUTEDThe approver said no; it ran anyway.
ARGS CHANGED AFTER APPROVALWhat ran isn’t what was approved.
APPROVAL EXPIRED / APPROVAL REUSEDToo late, or used twice.
SIGNED BY SEALING KEY / UNKNOWN APPROVERNot independent, or not a key you pinned.
AUTO-APPROVED (NOT HUMAN)A test or dev setting approved it. Never counts as a person.
approver key on agent hostSigned, but the key was on the vendor’s machine. Not independent.

Without --witness-log, order comes only from the vendor’s own record and is labelled “not independent”.

What a file proves — and what it doesn’t

  • Proves: the record has not changed since it was sealed, who sealed it and who approved it (once you have pinned their keys), and — with your log — that it is the version reported to you at the time.
  • Does not prove: that the AI’s decision was right, that every case was recorded, or that an approval came before the action. A file is evidence for your review, not a certificate. See the trust model and standards.
