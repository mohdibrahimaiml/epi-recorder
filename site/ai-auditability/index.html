AI Auditability

AI auditability starts with a file the auditor can check

Real AI auditability means a bank reviewer, insurance carrier, examiner, or opposing counsel can inspect a single decision six months later and verify independently that the record was not edited, reordered, or invented after the dispute began.

Why internal logs fail AI audits

Most teams treat logging or AI observability dashboards as an audit trail. When a regulated buyer asks “What did the model see on Claim 4471, which policy applied, and who approved the override?”, vendor-hosted logs fail three tests:

  • Custody bias: the party being audited owns the database, retention policy, and export script.
  • No tamper evidence: a CSV, JSON export, or PDF summary carries no hash chain or cryptographic signature.
  • Vendor lock-in: the auditor cannot check the evidence five years later if the SaaS account is closed.

What EPI seals for AI auditability

The open-source epi-recorder SDK and CLI seal each decision into one portable .epi archive containing:

  • Hash-chained timeline (steps.jsonl): inputs, tool calls, model ID/version, principal reasons, policy checks, and human approvals linked via prev_hash.
  • Cryptographic seal (manifest.json): SHA-256 digests over every member plus an Ed25519 signature.
  • Independent time proof: optional RFC 3161 timestamping (hash-only; file contents never leave your host) and witness log receipts to expose backdated re-signing.
  • Zero-account verification: reviewers run epi verify file.epi --policy strict locally or drop it into the offline browser verifier at epilabs.org/verify/.

What a seal proves — and what it does not

A passing verification proves the .epi file is intact and matches its pinned signer key. It does not prove the AI decision was legally compliant or that unrecorded code paths were captured. Read the three-layer trust model, standards mapping (EU AI Act Annex IV, NAIC, DORA, AIUC-1), and Auditor’s Guide before relying on any artifact in a formal review.
