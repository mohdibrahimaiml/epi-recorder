For reviewers

You received an AI decision file.
Check it yourself.

For the bank, insurer or lender whose AI vendor sends .epi decision files. Checking one is free, runs on your own machine, and needs no account with EPI or with the vendor.

Check a file in your browser One-page reviewer brief

What you receive

  • One file per case. What the AI decided, the principal reasons, the rules and data it relied on, which model answered, and who approved it.
  • Your own witness log. When the vendor seals each case, a short fingerprint of it arrives in a log that you keep. It holds no prompts and no personal data, and the vendor cannot change your copy.

Check one file — in a browser

Open epilabs.org/verify and drop the .epi file, plus your witness log (received.jsonl) if you have one. Nothing is uploaded. You will see whether the file has changed since it was sealed and, with the log, whether it is the version you were told about on the day — a rewritten and re-signed file reads “This is not the version your witness recorded.”

Check it properly — on your machine

The vendor sends you their public key, and each approver sends theirs. Confirm the fingerprints by phone, not by email, then:

$ pip install epi-recorder
$ epi keys trust vendor.pub --name vendor --fingerprint <from the vendor>
$ epi approvers add approver.pub --name alice@bank.com --fingerprint <from the approver>
$ epi verify case.epi --policy strict --require-human-approval --witness-log received.jsonl

DECISION: PASS means the file is unchanged, it was sealed by the vendor key you pinned, a person you pinned signed off, and it matches your log. Anything else tells you exactly what is missing. Details: reviewer brief.

The periodic review

For a quarter’s worth of cases, epi oversight report reads the files and your own log and writes one page: decisions by outcome, every model version that answered, who approved what, and the gaps — cases reported to you but never delivered, cases you expected that were never recorded, and files that don’t match your log. Available from epi-recorder 4.9 (how it works).

$ epi oversight report --cases ./received --log received.jsonl \
    --since 2026-07-01 --until 2026-09-30 --out oversight-q3.html

What a file proves — and what it doesn’t

  • Proves: the record has not changed since it was sealed, who sealed it and who approved it (once you have pinned their keys), and — with your log — that it is the version reported to you at the time.
  • Does not prove: that the AI’s decision was right, that every case was recorded, or that an approval came before the action. A file is evidence for your review, not a certificate. See the trust model and standards.
