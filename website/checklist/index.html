One record per decision

The AI decision record checklist.

Ten questions for any AI decision, then four more for insurance. Pick one real decision, answer from the record itself, and count the noes.

Download the one-page PDF Plain text

A record you can answer these questions from is a decision record. One you cannot is a log, a trace or a dashboard wearing a governance badge.

Every no is a gap.

The ten questions

Any AI decision.

  1. What was decided, in plain language?
  2. What outcome was recorded, and when?
  3. What inputs and data did the model rely on?
  4. Which model and version answered?
  5. What rules, policy or limit applied?
  6. What reasons were recorded for the decision?
  7. Who or what approved it, and when?
  8. If a human approval was required, is it attached to this record?
  9. What happened next — the action taken or the customer outcome?
  10. Can the reviewer read this without the vendor's system?
For insurance

Four more questions.

Carriers answering the NAIC's AI Risk Evaluation Supplement get asked a further four things that the general ten do not cover. The supplement is a draft; these are our questions, not its requirements.

  1. Did the reviewer agree with the AI, change it, or override it?
  2. Which version of the policy or rule was in force on the day of the decision?
  3. Was the record made at the time of the decision, not assembled later for an exam?
  4. Would the carrier notice if the record were changed after it was handed over?

Also worth asking, and not a question the vendor can answer alone: which decisions should have a record and do not? That list is usually the gap an examiner finds first. Read the insurance page for where each question comes from, or see a worked claim example.

How to score

One point per question answered from the record.

Score What it means
12–14A decision record a carrier can rely on.
8–11Close. Reasons, review or timing are incomplete.
4–7Observability data. You can debug it; a carrier cannot read it.
0–3Logs. The decision has to be reconstructed after the fact.
