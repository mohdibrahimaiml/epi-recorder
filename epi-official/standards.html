Standards

Standards support

What an .epi decision file can contribute when a customer answers to an auditor, a regulator or an underwriter - and what it cannot.

EVIDENCE INPUT, NOT A LEGAL CERTIFICATE · SPECIFICATION v4.8.0 · envelope-v2

EU AI Act - Annex IV technical documentation

epi enterprise pack assembles a per-system evidence pack: the sealed runs you select, their decision records, the manifest, and a reading guide. It is input to the technical documentation a provider must keep under Annex IV - not the document itself, and not a conformity assessment.

Article 12 logging (high-risk systems) applies from 2 December 2027; Annex I products from 2 August 2028. A sealed record is evidence that a given decision happened as recorded; it does not prove the system is compliant.

How the pack is built and verified →

AIUC-1 domains

epi verify --aiuc1 groups a run's sealed facts by AIUC-1 control domain so a reviewer can see which domains the record speaks to. It is a mapping aid: the control owner still decides whether the evidence satisfies the control.

SCITT and COSE receipts

Transparency receipts (SCITT/COSE Sign1) can be attached after sealing and re-signed. They are additive: removing or changing one is detectable, and a receipt never replaces the customer's own witness log.

What no standard requires

We found no rule that requires a digital signature on an AI decision record. What the regulations require is that the institution can explain a decision and show it kept the evidence - which is why the customer's copy matters more than the format.

Read the limits

Known limitations lists what a seal does and does not prove, including capture gaps and the difference between a signature and a witness.
