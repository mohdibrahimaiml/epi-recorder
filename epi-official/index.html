Who asks for this

Your customers already have to explain these decisions.

EPI is for AI companies selling decision-making agents to insurers, lenders and banks. When an applicant is turned down or a claim is refused, your customer has to say why — and these rules do not accept “the vendor’s model is proprietary” as the answer.

US lending

Specific reasons, even when a model decides

A lender that denies credit must give the applicant the specific, principal reasons. A vague statement or “didn’t meet our score” is not enough, and the rule has no exception for decisions made by a model.

ECOA · Regulation B, 12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2)

New York insurance

The vendor’s secrecy is not a defence

Insurers must keep documentation of the AI systems they use, including vendors’, and give the specific information behind a declination. They may not rely on a vendor’s proprietary algorithm to avoid being specific.

NYDFS Insurance Circular Letter No. 7 (2024)

European Union

An explanation of the decision itself

A person affected by an automated decision is entitled to an explanation of the procedure and principles actually applied to them. Trade secrets do not automatically override that right.

CJEU, Dun & Bradstreet Austria (C-203/22), 27 Feb 2025

EPI does not make anyone compliant. It gives your customer the record they need to answer the question — and you the proof that you handed it over. See Standards for what a record is, and is not.

What’s in a decision file

Six answers, sealed in one file.

Every case your agent decides becomes one .epi file. Here is the example on this page, claim CLM-1001, field by field. Change a single byte and the file no longer checks out.

Download the example Check it in your browser
01 · Outcome

What the AI decided

The action the agent actually took, as it took it: deny_claim on claim CLM-1001.

02 · Case

Which case it was

The case the run was about, so a reviewer can find it among thousands.

03 · Reasons

The principal reasons

In words a customer can pass on: water damage is excluded under HO3-4.2 for this peril.

04 · Basis

Rules and data relied on

The policy clause, the claim and the facts the decision rested on — the “source” regulators ask for.

05 · Model

Which model answered

The exact version that served the request, next to the one that was asked for — so a silent model change shows up.

06 · Approval

Who approved it

A person’s approval, signed with their own key — or plainly marked as missing. If the agent claims an approval itself, it is labelled as just that.

Your customer keeps a copy

A record you can’t quietly rewrite.

A signature protects the file — but you, the vendor, hold the signing key. So when each case is sealed, EPI also sends a short fingerprint of it to your customer’s own log. Only the fingerprint: no prompts, no personal data.

If a record is ever rewritten and signed again, the signature still checks out — but it no longer matches what your customer recorded on the day.

Your customer can run the log themselves, or use EPI Cloud’s hosted witness and share links (private beta).

On the day

The case is sealed

Your agent decides; the file is sealed and its fingerprint lands in your customer’s log.

Months later

Someone edits the record

The reason is changed and the file is re-signed with the vendor’s own, perfectly valid key.

At review

The forgery is caught

Your customer drops the file and their log on /verify: “This is not the version your witness recorded.”

Oversight report

The quarterly review, answered.

Banks and insurers don’t review one case at a time. They ask: over these three months, what did your AI decide, which model answered, who approved it — and what is missing? epi oversight report reads the decision files and your customer’s own log for the period and writes one page they can keep.

How it works

For the engineers: record, seal, check.

Watch a run being recorded, sealed and checked offline in 60 seconds. No server is needed to check a file: it carries Ed25519 signatures and SHA-256 hashes, and changing one byte breaks the seal. The full specification is in How it works and the Trust model.

Record it, seal it, prove it.

A dashboard log stays with whoever runs the dashboard. EPI turns each run into a file that leaves your systems and that anyone can check offline — your customer, their auditor, or a regulator.

Read the wire spec
Step 01 · Capture

Record it

Wrap OpenAI, Anthropic, LangChain, LiteLLM, or route through epi gateway serve. Every step is hash-chained as it executes, with secrets redacted by default.

Step 02 · Seal

Seal it

At run close, EPI seals the run into one file. By default a .epi is data-only: a plain ZIP of hash-linked steps and a manifest signed with Ed25519. Sealed with --with-viewer, the same file also opens in any browser.

Step 03 · Independent Check

Prove it

Auditors, underwriters, and bank reviewers check the file offline via epi verify --policy strict or inside a browser tab. Verification is MIT-licensed and free forever.

Honest Trust Boundary · Read Before Production

The seal proves integrity after sealing, not completeness of capture. In-process record() is self-attestation; use epi gateway serve for proxy-level capture. A .epi file is evidence input, not a regulatory certificate.

FAQ

  • EPI is open-source software that records what an AI agent saw, what it decided and why, which model answered and who approved it, and seals that into a portable .epi file signed with Ed25519. Anyone can check the file offline, without an account or a server.

  • A sealed .epi proves that after sealing, none of the member files or outer viewer HTML were altered, and the canonical manifest was signed by the Ed25519 private key matching the embedded public key. It does not by itself prove that every real-world call was captured, or who the signer is until you pin their key out of band (epi keys trust).

  • Because anyone can generate a fresh keypair and sign a file. A valid signature proves internal consistency, not identity. Until an auditor pins the vendor’s key fingerprint out of band with epi keys trust and runs epi verify --policy strict, EPI honestly reports NOT_PINNED (amber) rather than a misleading green checkmark.

  • Observability dashboards are hosted by the party being audited and require a login. EPI produces a portable, cryptographically sealed file that leaves your infrastructure and can be verified offline five years later by an insurer, bank reviewer, or regulator with open-source MIT tooling.

  • Run pip install epi-recorder && epi demo --no-browser to record, seal and verify your first decision offline in 60 seconds — or start a Decision File pilot (€4,500): EPI on one of your AI workflows: decision files for every case, and your customer keeps their own copy.
