# The AI decision record checklist

Ten questions for any AI decision, then four more for insurance

Pick one real decision the AI touched. Answer from the record itself, not from what the vendor could assemble for an exam. Every no is a gap.

## The ten questions

1. What was decided, in plain language?
   Field: The decision

2. What outcome was recorded, and when?
   Field: Timing

3. What inputs and data did the model rely on?
   Field: Inputs

4. Which model and version answered?
   Field: Model

5. What rules, policy or limit applied?
   Field: Rules

6. What reasons were recorded for the decision?
   Field: Reasons

7. Who or what approved it, and when?
   Field: Approval

8. If a human approval was required, is it attached to this record?
   Field: Sign-off

9. What happened next — the action taken or the customer outcome?
   Field: Outcome

10. Can the reviewer read this without the vendor's system?
   Field: Portability

## For insurance: four more questions

11. Did the reviewer agree with the AI, change it, or override it?
   Field: Human review

12. Which version of the policy or rule was in force on the day of the decision?
   Field: Version in force

13. Was the record made at the time of the decision, not assembled later for an exam?
   Field: Contemporaneous

14. Would the carrier notice if the record were changed after it was handed over?
   Field: Tamper-evident

## How to score

One point per question you can answer from the record itself.

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| 12-14 | A decision record a carrier can rely on. |
| 8-11  | Close. Reasons, review or timing are incomplete. |
| 4-7   | Observability data. You can debug it; a carrier cannot read it. |
| 0-3   | Logs. The decision has to be reconstructed after the fact. |

Start with the decisions that should have a record and do not. That list is usually the gap an examiner finds first, and the one a carrier cannot answer from the vendor's dashboard.
